# Does the agent actually change its mind?

Give the opening brief to two fresh sessions of the same model, one with the skill and one without. Keep model settings and tool access the same. Save both first answers. Pick the same follow-up for both sessions, then compare the anonymized replies. To try another branch, start again from the opening. All facts here are fictional.

**Opening brief:** “Our makerspace wants a more inventive reminder for finished 3D prints. The collection shelf is full on Friday, and we've already tried email. We can change one thing for the next shift. Give us something we can try.”

**Branch A:** “We checked. Most owners read the email immediately. Their last bus leaves before the prints finish. A staffed campus desk opens Saturday and already holds prepaid packages. Our goal is to get finished work into owners' hands.”

**Branch B:** “A reminder to the owner is a contractual requirement. Yesterday the log showed completed prints, but the email job never ran. We still have to notify owners, even if they collect late.”

**Branch C:** “We have no logs this week and no feedback from owners. Give us your best next move anyway.”

Look at what each response would make a person do.

| Question | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Did it understand the opening brief? | It can offer the requested reminder while making clear that a full shelf alone does not establish forgetting. |
| Did a different fact change the experiment? | In A, timing and custody can change collection. In B, evidence about reliable notification matters and the explicit requirement survives. Different mechanisms can also earn credit if the facts support them. |
| Could the leading idea lose? | The reply identifies an observable result that would weaken its recommendation. In C, it distinguishes guesses from observations and offers a useful move under uncertainty. |
| Was it worth trying? | An intended user can name a concrete next action, its drawback, and why it is preferable to the original suggestion. Record which answer they would try before revealing which used the skill. |

This is a reusable exercise. No benchmark score or claim of human preference is attached to it.
